## Atmospheric Reality Gap

Your homepage headline calls CH2O Inc. a "Global Innovator" in water treatment. Your stat counters display "0 B+ Gallons," "0 K+ Companies," "0 M+ USD in profit" — every metric rendering as zero because the JavaScript animation never resolves in a standard scrape, which is exactly how it renders for search bots and many slow-loading connections. The copy promises scale. The page delivers a column of zeros. That contradiction is not a design quirk; it is a credibility wound that opens before a buyer reads a single testimonial.

You have 10 content images on this homepage. Because none were attached to this audit, their visual quality is outside this scope — but their infrastructure is not, and that infrastructure has a 100% failure rate on alt text. Every one of those 10 images is invisible to search engines and inaccessible to screen-reader users. You are paying to host assets that contribute nothing to findability. That is a waste of bandwidth that also signals to compliance-minded enterprise buyers — the kind who run procurement checklists — that accessibility is not a priority here.

The trust cost: a buyer who lands from a referral and sees a zero-filled stat block next to untagged images does not think "technical glitch." They think "is this company still active?"

---

## Visual Narrative Coherence

Your link infrastructure is clean — 0 broken links across 25 checked — so the buyer's path does not collapse mid-journey. That is the floor, not the ceiling.

The fracture is structural: your page title is literally "Home" — four characters. No product category, no geography, no differentiator. When a procurement officer at a food-processing operation tabs between five vendor sites, your browser tab reads "Home." Every competitor with a real title owns that moment. You do not.

The "CLIENTS & FACTS" section appears twice in the page hierarchy with identical heading text. Duplicate section labels without distinct content signals a site assembled without a buyer's eye on the sequence. High-value industrial buyers — the category your testimonials from Stadelman Fruit and Pride Packing represent — compare vendors methodically. A page that repeats itself without resolving the repeated promise reads as unfinished, not thorough.

---

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

This is where the revenue leak is most measurable. You rank #1 organically for "CH2O Inc." — meaning buyers who already know your name find you. But your meta description is missing entirely, so Google writes that snippet for you, pulling random page text. You have zero control over the first sentence a searcher reads about your company before clicking. An og:image is also missing, which means every time a sales rep, client, or partner shares your URL on LinkedIn or via messaging, the link preview is imageless — a gray box where your brand should be.

You are winning the brand-name search and then handing the first impression to an algorithm. For a company citing millions in annual profit and clients across multiple agricultural and industrial sectors, that is not a resource constraint — it is an unforced error that costs you every share, every forward, every second-screen lookup during a vendor-review meeting.

Fix the meta description. Set an og:image. Give your title tag a real name. These changes take hours and immediately change what buyers see before they arrive.
